- covariance matrix
   - random vector is just vector made of random variables, the expectation of the vector is just the expectation of the components
   - omdat $\mathbb E[Ax] = A\mathbb E[x]$ werk dat dus ook met een matrix
   - $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \to \mathbb E[Ax] = \mathbb E[\begin{bmatrix} 1x + 2y \\ 2x -1y\end{bmatrix}] = \begin{bmatrix}1 \mathbb E[x] + 2 \mathbb E[y] \\ 2 \mathbb E[x] -1 \mathbb E [y]\end{bmatrix}$
   - this is not tricky but the variance is
   - $var(x) = \mathbb E[(X-\mathbb E[X])^2]$
   - issue is no square of vector, hence we take the dot product
   - (recall dot product is just $X^T \times X$ (transpose)
   - therefore $var(\vec X) = var(x_1) + var(x_2) + \dots + var(x_n)$
   - this has issues because you lose info
   - don't take $x^T \times x$ which gives a number, instead take $X \times x^T$ which gives a matrix.... a covariance matrix
   - taking variance of 2d vector. $\begin{bmatrix}var(x_1) & cov(x_1, x_2) \\ cov(x_2, x_1) & var(x_2)\end{bmatrix}$, covariance matrix of $\vec X$ is noted as $\Sigma(\vec X)$
   - $cov(x_1, x_2) = cov(x_2, x_1)$ aldus altijd symetrische matrix
   - $\Sigma(A\vec X) = A\Sigma(\vec X)A^T$
