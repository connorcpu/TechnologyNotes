## change of units formula
- for (linearly) converting the variance of a random variable to another variable using the relation F = aC + b
- $var(F) = a^2 \cdot var(C)$
   - want: $E[ax+b] = a E[x] + b \land var(ax+b) = E[((ax+b) - E[ax+b])^2]$
   - dus: $E[ax-a E[x]) + (b-b)^2$
   - dus dus: $E[a^2(x -E[x])^2] = a^2 E[(x-E[x])^2]$
- willkeurig: $E[x^2] \geq (E[x])^2$
   - $E[(x-E[x])^2] = E[x^2] - (E[x])^2$
   - since beide $\geq 0$ zijn, aldus de stelling

## standardization of the normal
- suppose $x ~ N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ dan is $rX+s$ ook normaal verdeeld
- $rX+s ~ N(r\mu + s, r^2 \sigma^2)$
- therefore: $Z = {X - \mu \over \sigma}$, Z is then standard normal, X is the one you are trying to find
- example: $P(x \leq 7) = P(X-5 \leq 2) = P({{x-5} \over 2} \leq 1) = P(Z \leq 1)$


## variance of the sums
- verwachting van het gemiddelde van random variables is de $\mu$
- $var(x+y) = var(x) + var(y)$ (als x & y onafhankelijk)
- $var(x-y) = var(x) + var(y)$
- dus de variantie van het gemiddelde zal steeds kleiner worden: $\sigma^2 \over n$ (waar n hoeveelheid proeven is)
- variantie moet eindig zijn

- var(X + Y) = var(X) + var(Y)
- if i average n realisations of an RV X ($\bar X_n$) the variance ofcourse reduces, therefore $var(\bar X_n) = {\sigma^2 \over n}$

## law of large numbers
- just apply a limit, since n to infinity, average pulls to mu

## sample variance and sample standard deviation
- sample variance is $S_n^2 = {1 \over n-1} \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar X_n)^2$
- this is the emperical variance and the formula is 100% acurate (because it's emperical) so it's an 'unbiased estimator'

## mean squared error
- $MSE_\theta (\hat \theta) = (bias(\hat\theta))^2 + var(\hat\theta)$
- MSE considers both bias (towards the mean) and the variance.
- it ballances the 2, therefore lower mean squared error is always best
- the larger the number of samples you calculate the MSE over the lower the bias ofcourse
- therefore by the law of large numbers, MSE(X) = var(X)
