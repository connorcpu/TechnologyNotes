   - this means of discrete variable it is sufficient to prove one of the above statements is true for every outcome
   - for continues random variables, X and Y are independed if they are independed for all $X \leq x \land Y \leq y$ (what??) 
- expectiation of continous van X with pdf p(X) is given by $\displaystyle \mathbb E[X]= \int_{-\infty}^\infty x p(x)dx$ E[X] is the median or the expectation
- expected value of discrete var is the sum of all options times their change: for dice, 1/6 * 1 + 1/6 * 2
   - the expectiation should roughly be the same as the average but not nececiarly the median 
   - X^2 does not mean E[X^2] = (E[X])^2 because the whole PDF changes so the weight shifts with further from zero getting scaled more
   - change-of-variable applies when you apply a $\R \to \R$ function to an RV
   - say Y = g(X) then the distribution of Y $F_Y(x) = P(Y \leq x) = P(g(X) \leq x)$
   - then the PDF is 
      - $E[g(X)] = \sum_i g(a_i)P(X = a_i)$
      - $\mathbb E[g(X)] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty g(x)f(x) dx$ where f(x) the PDF of the original function was
   - in geval g(x) is linear -> $\mathbb E [c_1 + c_2 x] = \sum_{a_i} (c_1 + c_2a_i)\cdot p(x=a_i)$
      - $c_1 + c_2 \cdot \mathbb E[x]$
   - for linear change of formula, or change-of-unit, you can just assume hyper-linearity:
