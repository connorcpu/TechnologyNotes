# newtons method (newton-raphson)
$$x_n = x_{n-1} - {{f(x_{n-1})}\over{f'(x_{n-1})}}$$
to approximate any expression, convert to a function in such a matter that f(x) = 0 will give you a usefull x   
do this by just saying x = a, when looking for a. then derive f(x) = 0
1. to find $f(x)$, say $x=\sqrt[3]2 \land f(x) = 0$
   - so $x = \sqrt[3]2$ thus $x^3 = 2$
   - as such, $f(x) = x^3 - 2 = 0$
   - $f'(x) = 3x^2$
   - when $f(x) = 0; x = \sqrt[3]2$
2. take a wild guess, in this case, $x_0 = 1$
3. fill in the newton-raphson equation: $x_1 = 1 - {{1^3 - 2}\over{3 \cdot 1^2}} \to x_1 = 1 - \frac {-1}{3} \to x_1 = \frac 4 3$
4. repeat
$$\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infin}x_n = \alpha$$ 
where $\alpha$ is the solution to what you are trying to approximate
